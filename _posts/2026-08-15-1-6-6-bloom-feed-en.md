1:6:6 is a bloom feed — low nitrogen, heavy phosphorus and potassium, named for its N : P₂O₅ : K₂O ratio. The simplest way to build it is three products:

| Per 50 litres | |
|---|---|
| Mono potassium phosphate (MKP) | 36.90 g |
| Ammonium nitrate 34-0-0 | 9.10 g |
| Potassium sulphate (SOP) | 13.60 g |

That delivers N 61.9, P 167.5, K 321.2 and S 46.2 ppm, with the nitrogen split exactly half ammonium and half nitrate.

The problem is the middle line. **Ammonium nitrate is a regulated substance in India** and awkward to buy in small quantities. So the real question is how to build the same feed without it — and the answer is not simply to drop it, because that would take the ammonium with it.

**Set the sliders below and the recipe rebuilds itself.** Whichever route you pick, the ammonium : nitrate ratio stays locked at 1 : 1.

## Why Four Routes, Not One

The rest of this article works through why the ratio matters, what each route costs you, and where the two extra sliders hit their limits.

## The Ratio Is the Thing to Protect

Ammonium nitrate is convenient because it delivers ammonium and nitrate in one product, in exactly equal amounts. Swap it for anything else and you have to replace **both halves separately**, or the nitrogen character of the feed changes even though the total N stays the same.

That matters more than the total. Ammonium acidifies the root zone as roots take it up; nitrate does not. A feed that drifts from 50:50 to 20:80 is a different feed agronomically, even if the label still reads 1:6:6.

So every route below replaces the ammonium with one product and the nitrate with potassium nitrate, keeping the split at 1:1.

## Four Routes to the Same Solution

All quantities per 50 litres, all delivering the same N, P and K:

| Route | MKP | MAP | Ammonium sulphate | Ammonium nitrate | Potassium nitrate | SOP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ammonium nitrate** (reference) | 36.90 g | — | — | 9.10 g | — | 13.60 g |
| **MAP** | 21.78 g | 12.89 g | — | — | 11.29 g | 13.43 g |
| **Ammonium sulphate** | 36.90 g | — | 7.37 g | — | 11.29 g | 3.14 g |
| **Ready-made 13:40:13** (17.99 g) | 23.06 g | — | — | — | 5.51 g | 13.23 g |

Notice what happens in each case:

- **MAP** carries phosphorus in with the ammonium, so the MKP drops by about 40% to make room. This is the tidiest substitute — MAP brings no sulphate, so sulphur stays where it was.
- **Ammonium sulphate** brings sulphate instead, which is why the potassium sulphate collapses from 13.60 g to 3.14 g. The sulphur is simply arriving in a different bag.
- Both routes need **potassium nitrate** to replace the nitrate half that the ammonium nitrate used to supply. That is the step people forget.

The delivered N, P and K are identical across all four. Sulphur varies by less than 1 ppm. The choice is purely about what you can buy.

## Using Ready-Made 13:40:13

You can build most of this from a bag of 13:40:13 instead of separate salts, and the calculator has a slider for it. But there is a ceiling, and it is set by the nitrogen form.

**13:40:13 is 13% N, of which 8.6 points are ammonium and 4.4 are nitrate — so 66% of its nitrogen is ammonium**, where this blend wants 50%. Push it too far and the feed becomes ammonium-dominant.

The cap works out at **0.36 g/L (17.99 g per 50 L)**. At exactly that dose it supplies *all* of the ammonium and about half of the nitrate, and the remaining nitrate comes from potassium nitrate. Past that point the ratio breaks, so the slider stops there.

That maximum setting is worth knowing: it needs no ammonium nitrate, no MAP and no ammonium sulphate — just 13:40:13, MKP, potassium nitrate and SOP, four ordinary products. It is also the most ion-balanced version of the blend, at 0.03%.

## The Pekacid Slider

Pekacid 0-60-20 can supply phosphorus and potassium here too, and it acidifies at the same time. The slider runs from 0 up to **0.64 g/L**, where it supplies all the phosphorus and the MKP disappears entirely.

Two things happen as you push it up, and both are worth watching:

**Sulphur climbs, from 46 to 88 ppm.** Pekacid carries less potassium per unit of phosphorus than the MKP it replaces (it is half potassium phosphate, half phosphoric acid), so potassium sulphate has to make up the potassium — and sulphate comes along with it.

**The acid load gets serious.** At the top of the slider Pekacid releases about **2.7 meq/L of free acid**. For comparison, the entire alkalinity of typical treated borewell water is around 0.6 meq/L. Running the slider near maximum without knowing your water will drive the pH down hard and fast. Treat anything above about 0.25 g/L as something to approach with a pH meter in hand.

## What This Blend Is Not

1:6:6 has **no calcium, no magnesium and no micronutrients**. It is a bloom booster, not a complete diet. Run it alongside your normal feed rather than in place of it, or you will produce a calcium deficiency in a few weeks and wonder why the new growth is deformed.

One handling note: if you take the ammonium sulphate route, do not dry-blend it with any calcium-bearing fertiliser. Add them to the tank separately.

## Practical Summary

- **The reference blend is MKP 36.90 g + ammonium nitrate 9.10 g + SOP 13.60 g per 50 L.**
- **To drop the ammonium nitrate, replace both halves:** an ammonium source (MAP or ammonium sulphate) *and* potassium nitrate for the nitrate. Replacing only one half changes the feed.
- **MAP is the tidiest substitute.** Ammonium sulphate works equally well and is usually cheaper and easier to find.
- **13:40:13 caps at 17.99 g per 50 L** because its nitrogen is 66% ammonium against the 50% this blend needs.
- **Keep Pekacid modest** unless you have measured your water. The phosphorus ceiling is 0.64 g/L, but the acid load bites long before that.
