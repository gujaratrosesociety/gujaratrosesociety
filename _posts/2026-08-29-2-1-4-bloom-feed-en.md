This is the **2:1:4** bloom feed — named for the approximate nitrogen to phosphorus to potassium ratio it delivers. The target nutrient profile, as ppm ranges:

| Nutrient | Amount (ppm) |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N) | 120–140 |
| Phosphorus (P) | 50–70 |
| Potassium (K) | 250–300 |
| Calcium (Ca) | 140–170 |
| Magnesium (Mg) | 50–70 |
| Sulfur (S) | 60–80 |

Growers commonly push potassium up and nitrogen down relative to a general-purpose feed once buds are setting and flowers are developing — potassium drives sugar transport and cell turgor, both of which matter more for bloom quality than for vegetative push. This tool builds that profile the same way the [4:1:4 rose feed]({{ site.baseurl }}/nutrition/fertilizers/phosphorus/ion-balance/2026/08/15/4-1-4-rose-feed-en.html) calculator does: from ICL Pekacid, calcium nitrate, magnesium sulphate, potassium nitrate and potassium sulphate, fully ion balanced.

**Move the sliders below and the complete recipe calculates itself.**

Every combination the tool produces holds potassium, calcium, magnesium and phosphorus exactly on target, and the ion balance always closes. The targets used are the midpoints of the ranges above: N 130, P 60, K 275, Ca 155, Mg 60, S 70 ppm.

If you only take one setting away: **Pekacid at its maximum dose, ammonium at 8%.**

There is one thing this recipe cannot do, and it is worth reading before you use it: **it cannot hit the sulfur target.** The section below explains why, because it is not a bug in the tool — it is a property of asking for this much calcium and potassium at once.

## What Pekacid Actually Contains

Pekacid is a co-crystal of mono potassium phosphate and phosphoric acid (KH₂PO₄·H₃PO₄). Per **gram** of product it gives you:

| Delivers | Per gram |
|---|---|
| Phosphorus (P) | 8.45 mmol (26.2%) |
| Potassium (K) | 4.25 mmol (16.6%) |
| Free acid (H⁺) | 4.21 meq |

That free acid counts as a **cation** in an ion balance sheet, so every gram of Pekacid needs a matching extra anion somewhere else in the recipe — the same fact that drives the sulfur story below.

## The Ceiling: Phosphorus, Not pH

**Maximum Pekacid = 0.229 g/L = 2.29 g per 10 litres.**

The bloom target is 60 ppm (1.94 mmol/L) of P. Divide that by the 8.45 mmol of P in a gram of Pekacid and you get 0.229 g/L. This is a noticeably higher ceiling than the 4:1:4 feed's 0.148 g/L, because this profile asks for more phosphorus (60 ppm against 39 ppm). Past 0.229 g/L there is nothing you can do — phosphorus cannot be taken back out of solution once it is in the tank, and excess phosphorus will lock up iron and zinc and throw calcium phosphate at the concentrations this recipe runs at.

As in the 4:1:4 feed, only Pekacid, MKP and potassium sulphate move against the Pekacid dial. **Use the maximum dose** — it needs no MKP, which is one less product to weigh, and the EC barely moves across the whole range: Pekacid is a cheap dial here too.

## Why Sulfur Can't Hit Target

This is the part that makes 2:1:4 different from the 4:1:4 feed, not just a rescaled copy of it.

The calculator opens at a gentler **1.00 mS/cm** by default, but the sulfur overshoot is proportional — it does not go away at lower strength. At the full-strength target EC (**2.61 mS/cm**, the setting that matches the published ppm targets above), the tool delivers **~175 ppm of sulfur against a 70 ppm target** — about two and a half times over. Push the Pekacid dose down to zero and it still delivers ~150 ppm. There is no slider position, at any strength, that brings the ratio down to target. Here is why.

Sulfate is the only anion left in this recipe once nitrate and phosphate are spoken for, and it has to absorb whatever charge is left over. Calcium nitrate is fixed by the calcium target (155 ppm = 3.87 mmol/L), and it drags in 2.2 mmol of nitrate for every mmol of calcium — **8.5 mmol/L of nitrate**, whether the recipe wants that much or not. Potassium is fixed at 275 ppm = 7.03 mmol/L, magnesium at 60 ppm = 2.47 mmol/L, and Pekacid's free acid adds another ~1 meq/L of cation at full dose. Add it up: the cations (NH₄, K, 2×Ca, 2×Mg, H⁺) come to roughly **21.6 meq/L**, and the only anions available to balance them besides sulfate — nitrate and phosphate — cover barely half of that. Sulfate has to make up the rest, and at these concentrations that lands at 150–180 ppm depending on the ammonium split, not 70.

This is a direct consequence of asking for **both** high calcium and high potassium in the same recipe while nitrate is pinned down by calcium: there simply isn't enough non-sulfate anion capacity in this product set to close the balance any other way. The 4:1:4 feed has the same mechanism but a much smaller effect (58–61 ppm delivered against a 51 ppm target) because its Ca and K targets are both lower.

**What this means in practice:** treat the 60–80 ppm sulfur range in the target profile as aspirational with these products, not as something this recipe will hit. If sulfur at 150–180 ppm is a genuine concern for your water and substrate, the fix is outside the scope of this calculator — it means bringing in some potassium or calcium as a chloride salt instead of sulphate/nitrate, which trades one problem for a chloride-tolerance question. For most fertigation setups, sulfate at this level is not toxic on its own, but it is worth knowing it is happening rather than assuming the recipe missed its target by mistake.

## Table 2: Raising Ammonium

Ammonium comes first from calcium nitrate itself — the double salt inherently carries 0.2 mmol of ammonium per mmol of calcium — then from potassium nitrate's nitrate budget, then from ammonium sulphate once that budget runs out. Total N, K, Ca, Mg and P stay on target up to the natural floor; past it, calcium nitrate is already forcing more nitrate into the tank than the requested split allows, and total N is pushed upward to compensate.

**That floor arrives at 8.3% ammonium in this feed** — much sooner than the 4:1:4 feed's ~6%, because calcium is higher here (155 ppm vs 136 ppm) while total nitrogen is lower (130 ppm vs 160 ppm), so calcium nitrate's forced nitrate eats a larger share of the nitrogen budget.

| NH₄ % of total N | Calcium nitrate blend | Potassium nitrate | Ammonium sulphate | Total N | Sulphur | Est. EC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | caTet 9.13 g | 1.56 g | — | 130 ppm | 152 ppm | 2.50 |
| 3% | ca 3.01 g + caTet 5.85 g | 1.00 g | — | 130 ppm | 160 ppm | 2.54 |
| 6% | ca 6.02 g + caTet 2.56 g | 0.44 g | — | 130 ppm | 169 ppm | 2.58 |
| **8.3% (floor)** | ca 8.36 g (all Calcinit) | ~0 g | — | 130 ppm | 176 ppm | 2.61 |
| 10% | ca 8.36 g | — | 0.11 g | **132 ppm** | 179 ppm | 2.64 |
| 20% | ca 8.36 g | — | 0.89 g | **149 ppm** | 198 ppm | 2.80 |
| 30% | ca 8.36 g | — | 1.90 g | **170 ppm** | 222 ppm | 3.00 |
| 40% | ca 8.36 g | — | 3.24 g | **199 ppm** | 255 ppm | 3.27 |

Pekacid is fixed at 2.29 g and magnesium sulphate at 6.08 g in every row above.

**Read this table carefully.** Above 8.3% ammonium, total nitrogen is no longer on target — it climbs steadily, reaching 25% over target at 30% ammonium. The published guidance for roses caps ammonium at 5–15% of total N, with a hard limit of 1.5 mmol/L (21 ppm as NH₄-N); only the rows up to about 10% stay inside that. Treat 20% and up as a demonstration of where the chemistry runs out of room, not as a feeding plan.

## Ammonium Nitrate or Ammonium Sulphate? Your Choice

Same as the 4:1:4 feed: the third slider lets you choose which product carries any ammonium beyond what calcium nitrate already supplies, and the delivered solution does not change either way. At settings below the 8.3% floor this slider has nothing to do — calcium nitrate is already supplying all the ammonium the recipe wants — which is why it locks near the default. Above the floor, ammonium sulphate is usually the practical choice in India, since ammonium nitrate is a regulated substance and often hard to buy in small quantities.

One handling note carried over unchanged: **never dry-blend ammonium sulphate with calcium nitrate.** Add them to the tank separately.

## Both Dials at Once

As with the 4:1:4 feed, the Pekacid dial and the ammonium dial don't interact — moving Pekacid only touches Pekacid, MKP and potassium sulphate; moving ammonium only touches calcium nitrate/tetrahydrate, potassium nitrate, ammonium sulphate and potassium sulphate. Open **"Show the full grid"** in the tool to see both at once: read left to right and almost nothing changes; read top to bottom and the grid runs into warning colours fast, because in this feed even the *floor* ammonium setting already delivers sulfur well above target — there's very little green in this grid compared to the 4:1:4 one, and that is the correct picture, not a display bug.

## Micronutrients (Same as the 4:1:4 Feed)

Micronutrient dosing is stage-independent — the same stock works here. Per 10 litres:

| Product | Amount |
|---|---|
| Fe-DTPA 6% | 326 mg |
| Mn-EDTA 13% | 29.6 mg |
| Boric acid 17.5% B | 21.6 mg |
| Zn-EDTA 15% | 15.3 mg |
| Cu-EDTA 15% | 4.7 mg |
| Sodium molybdate 39.6% | 2.4 mg |

Make a **100× stock** — ten times the above quantities in 1 litre of water, dosed at 100 mL per 10 litre batch — rather than trying to weigh milligram quantities directly. Use Fe-DTPA, not Fe-EDDHA, for the same reason as the 4:1:4 feed: EDDHA breaks down near the pH right next to a dissolving Pekacid particle.

## Where This Feed Fits

The [1:6:6 basal feed]({{ site.baseurl }}/nutrition/fertilizers/phosphorus/potassium/2026/08/15/1-6-6-basal-feed-en.html) is explicitly a start-of-cycle PK dose with no calcium, magnesium or micronutrients, meant to be followed by a complete feeding programme once the new flush is underway. The [4:1:4 feed]({{ site.baseurl }}/nutrition/fertilizers/phosphorus/ion-balance/2026/08/15/4-1-4-rose-feed-en.html) is that kind of complete, general-purpose feed. This 2:1:4 profile, with potassium pushed well above nitrogen, fits the pattern many growers use once buds are visible and flowers are developing — but that staging isn't something this site has laid out as a fixed programme, so treat it as a target profile to reach for during bloom, and use your own judgement (or your water and substrate testing) for exactly when to switch.

## Practical Summary

- **Pekacid maxes out at 2.29 g per 10 litres** — a phosphorus limit, higher than the 4:1:4 feed's because this profile targets more P.
- **Use the maximum Pekacid dose.** It costs almost nothing in EC and needs no MKP.
- **Sulfur cannot reach the 60–80 ppm target with these products** — expect 150–180 ppm depending on ammonium. This is forced by the combination of high calcium and high potassium with nitrate pinned by calcium; it is not a calculator error.
- **The ammonium floor is 8.3%**, noticeably higher than the 4:1:4 feed's ~6% — go above it and total nitrogen starts climbing.
- **The two dials are independent**, same as the 4:1:4 feed, but this grid runs into warnings much sooner because the sulfur overshoot is already large at the default settings.
- **Always dissolve to the full final volume.** Calcium phosphate that forms in a concentrated tank does not redissolve.
